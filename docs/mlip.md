# Data from ASE calculators

`discopt.calphad.mlip` (issue #5) produces CALPHAD data from **any ASE
calculator**: a machine-learned potential (UMA, MACE, MTP, ...), a classical
one (EAM, EMT), or anything else that returns energies and forces. Choosing a
model is one line, and nothing in the package names a model.

```bash
pip install "discopt-calphad[sqs]"   # ase, spglib and icet; the model package is yours to install
```

## The calculator produces data, not phase models

```
structures --calculator--> energies --> Dataset --fit()--> ThermoDatabase / TDB --> certified equilibria
```

A calculator is never used as a phase model inside `equilibrium()`. A
certificate needs every phase's Gibbs energy in three forms (a number, a
discopt expression and interval bounds), and a neural network has no
algebraic form in site-fraction space, so it has no rigorous lower bound
either. A calculator also gives the energies of specific configurations at
0 K, while a CALPHAD phase needs G(y, T) everywhere. The CALPHAD parameters
are the interpolant between the computed configurations.

!!! warning "What the certificate covers"
    A fit to calculated data, and every certified equilibrium computed from
    it, are exact statements about the **fitted model**. They say nothing about
    how good the calculator is. The calculator's error reaches the result only
    through the σ of each data point. Compare its values with an assessment
    or with experiments (as the Al–Cu example does) to know that error.

## What is computed

- **Formation enthalpies** (`HM_FORM`) of compounds and CEF endmembers, per
  mole of atoms, relative to each element relaxed in the structure of its
  reference phase (the database's ELEMENT line), with the same model. Errors
  common to the compound and the elements largely cancel.
- **Mixing enthalpies** (`HM_MIX`) of a solution phase on its own lattice,
  relative to the phase's pure endmembers, from special quasirandom structures
  generated by icet (`method="sqs"`). Each composition gets several SQS in
  different supercells.

A 0 K energy stands in for the enthalpy at 298.15 K. Every observation
records this in its `note`, and `FitResult.to_tdb()` repeats it in the header.
The pure elements are never fitted: the SGTE unaries stay as they are.

```python
from ase.calculators.emt import EMT
from discopt.calphad import databases, mlip
from discopt.calphad.fit import fit

db = databases.load("alcuy_zhang")
model = mlip.from_ase(EMT(), name="EMT", cache=True)
result = mlip.compute(
    model, db, ("AL", "CU"),
    compounds={"ALCU_THETA": ["AL:CU"]},
    structures={"ALCU_THETA": ("C16", ["8h"], ["4a"])},
    mixing={"FCC_A1": [0.25, 0.5, 0.75]},
    model_sigma=2.0,
)
print(result.table())
res = fit(db, result.dataset, ("AL", "CU"), {
    "L(FCC_A1,AL,CU:VA;0)": "a + 2*T",
    "G(ALCU_THETA,AL:CU;0)": "a + 6.75*T + 2*GHSERAL + GHSERCU",
})
```

With UMA the only change is the calculator:

```python
from fairchem.core import FAIRChemCalculator, pretrained_mlip

predictor = pretrained_mlip.get_predict_unit("uma-s-1p1", device="cpu")
model = mlip.from_ase(FAIRChemCalculator(predictor, task_name="omat"), name="uma-s-1p1/omat", cache=True)
```

## Structures

`mlip.PROTOTYPES` holds the prototypes, each defined by its space group and
Wyckoff sites:

- A1, A2, A3, A4, B2, L1₂, D0₁₉, C14, C15, C36, C16 and D8_b (σ);
- from the [AFLOW Encyclopedia of Crystallographic Prototypes](https://aflow.org/prototype-encyclopedia/),
  with its published coordinates: D0₂₀ (Al₃Ni), D0₁₁ (Fe₃C), D5₁₃ (Al₃Ni₂),
  D0₂₂ (Al₃Ti), D0₂₃ (Al₃Zr), D0₃ (BiF₃, Fe₃Al), L1₀ (CuAu), B19 (AuCd), C11_b
  (MoSi₂), B8₁ (NiAs) and Ga₃Pt₅ (Al₃Ni₅).
`mlip.PHASES` maps the generic TDB names (FCC_A1, BCC_A2, HCP_A3, DIAMOND_A4,
B2_BCC, L12_FCC, D019_HCP, LAVES_C14/C15/C36 and their C15_LAVES spellings,
SIGMA in the 10:4:16 model) to a prototype and a sublattice-to-site map.
Database names are not standardized, so any other phase takes a map from the
user, as `ALCU_THETA` does above. A structure that no prototype covers is given
as `(ase.Atoms, atom indices per sublattice)`, or as
`PhaseStructure.from_atoms(atoms, sublattices)`. A CIF file works directly:
`PhaseStructure.from_cif(path, [["Al"], ["Ni"]])`, selecting sites by element
or by CIF site label. The map is checked against the phase's site ratios
before anything is computed.

SQS mixing needs one sublattice that mixes the two elements, with every
other sublattice fixed. That sublattice may also allow vacancies; the SQS
then mix the elements at zero vacancy fraction. The mixing enthalpy of a
multi-sublattice phase is taken relative to the two endmembers of its mixed
sublattice, and `fit` uses it on the same terms.

An order–disorder phase (L1₂, B2) is unsupported as such. Rewrite it as a
plain phase with `discopt.calphad.ordering.stand_in` before computing data
for it.

Before fitting, compare the calculator with the starting database:
`discopt.calphad.fit.predict(db, result.dataset, elements)` gives the
database's value for every observation. A calculator that gets signs or tens
of kJ/mol wrong cannot describe the system. ASE's EMT, for example, gives
positive formation enthalpies for Al–Cu and Ni–Al. `enthalpy_form(db, label)`
writes the form to fit to such data: a free constant, with the database's
T-dependence kept.

## Relaxation, and leaving the prototype

`RelaxPolicy(mode=...)` chooses between:

- `"symmetry"` (default): positions and cell relax, constrained to the
  starting space group;
- `"full"`: positions and cell relax freely;
- `"fixed"`: the energy of the structure as built.

`fmax`, `steps` and `optimizer` go to the ASE optimizer. A relaxation can
carry a structure into another one: BCC collapsing to FCC along the Bain path,
or a Laves phase distorting. Each `Relaxation` records three measures, and
sets `left_prototype` with the reason when one crosses its threshold:

- the cell strain that breaks the symmetry of the parent lattice. The parent
  lattice is the structure with every atom taken as one species, so an SQS is
  judged by its FCC or BCC lattice. A change of c/a keeps a hexagonal cell's
  symmetry and does not count; a shear of a cubic cell does;
- the largest atomic displacement, as a fraction of the nearest-neighbour
  distance;
- the space group before and after.

A non-converged relaxation is also flagged. Flagged structures are listed in
`MLIPResult.flagged` and left out of the dataset. They are never fitted
silently.

## σ

With `sigma="spread"` (the default), σ has two parts, added in quadrature:
σ² = σ_config² + σ_model².

**σ_config** is the spread of the SQS behind a mixing enthalpy. The standard
deviation of n structures has relative standard error about 1/√(2(n − 1)).
Two SQS give a single degree of freedom, and the true σ can then lie anywhere
from about half to thirty times the estimate. So by default (`pool=True`) the
variances of all compositions of a phase are pooled, Σ(n_i − 1)s_i² /
Σ(n_i − 1). Nine compositions with two SQS each give one σ_config with nine
degrees of freedom, good to about 25%. `pool=False` keeps one σ per
composition. Raising `SQSOptions(count=)` improves either; about 7 structures
per composition give 30% on their own, and 13 give 20%. SQS are optimized
structures, not random draws, so their spread is mostly a finite-size effect
and tends to understate the configurational variance.

**Random decorations** (`method="random"`, settings in
`mlip.RandomOptions(count=8, min_atoms=16, max_atoms=64, seed=0)`) average
the mixing enthalpy over `count` independent random decorations of a
supercell that holds the composition exactly. No SQS tool is needed. The value
is their mean, and σ_config is its **standard error**, s/√n: the draws are
independent samples of the random-alloy average, so more draws shrink it. The
spread s is pooled over a phase's compositions by default, as for SQS.
Relaxing random structures moves atoms more than relaxing SQS does, so the
prototype check flags more of them.

**Cluster expansion** (`method="cluster_expansion"`, settings in
`mlip.ClusterExpansionOptions(cutoffs=(2.1, 1.5), max_size=4,
max_structures=40, fit_method="least-squares", n_splits=5, seed=0)`) hands
the expansion to icet; discopt-calphad only moves data between it and the
calculator. icet enumerates every decoration of the lattice in supercells of
up to `max_size` primitive cells (29 for FCC at size 4; a random subset of
`max_structures`, endmembers always kept), the calculator relaxes each at its
Vegard volume, and each gives a mixing energy relative to the relaxed
endmembers. icet's trainstation fits the effective cluster interactions with
`n_splits`-fold cross validation, and the expansion then predicts the
**ideal random alloy** at each grid composition from icet's random-alloy
cluster vector, with no sampling noise. Any composition works, not only those
a small supercell can hold. σ_config is the cross-validation RMSE, the
expected error for a structure the fit has not seen; an exact fit (round-off
below 1 µeV/atom) has none, and then `model_sigma=` is required. The fit is
kept in `MLIPResult.cluster_expansions[phase]`
(`ClusterExpansionFit`: `.expansion` is the icet object, `.predict(atoms)`,
`.random_alloy(y)`, `.summary()`). Training structures that leave the
lattice on relaxation are flagged and not used. All grid points come from one
expansion, so their errors are correlated, which a fit treating them as
independent does not see.

For EMT Cu–Au, 29 structures give a CV RMSE of 14 meV/atom (1.3 kJ/mol), and
the predicted ΔH_mix at y = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 (1.1, 1.3, 1.2 kJ/mol) agree with
ten random 32-atom decorations (0.6, 1.5, 1.2 kJ/mol) within that σ. For a
pure nearest-neighbour pair calculator, the expansion is exact.

**Short-range order** (`monte_carlo=mlip.MonteCarloOptions(temperatures=(600,
900, 1200), min_atoms=256, sweeps=200, equilibration=0.25, sro_radius=1.1,
integrate=True, n_beta=12, seed=0)`, with `method="cluster_expansion"`). A
random alloy has no short-range order, but a real one at finite temperature
prefers like or unlike neighbours, which lowers its enthalpy and its entropy
of mixing. With `monte_carlo=`, icet's mchammer samples the canonical
ensemble of the cluster expansion in an n×n×n supercell at each temperature,
and the data become:

- `HM_MIX` at each temperature: ⟨ΔH_mix⟩, with σ² = CV RMSE² + (statistical
  error)² + σ_model²; the statistical error is mchammer's, which accounts for
  the correlation length of the run;
- `SM_MIX` at each temperature (with `integrate=True`), by thermodynamic
  integration in β = 1/kT from infinite temperature, where the alloy is random
  and its entropy ideal: βΔG(β) = s_mix(y ln y + (1 − y) ln(1 − y)) +
  ∫₀^β ⟨ΔH⟩ dβ′, and ΔS = (⟨ΔH⟩ − ΔG)/T. At β = 0, ⟨ΔH⟩ is the
  expansion's random-alloy value, exactly. The rest of the integral comes from
  `n_beta` extra Monte Carlo runs and the trapezoid rule. σ is the statistical
  error carried through the integral, plus the energy σ divided by T (an
  estimate); `entropy_sigma=` replaces it.

The Warren–Cowley parameters α of each pair shell within `sro_radius`
nearest-neighbour distances are in `MLIPResult.short_range_order[phase]`
(`SROResult`: `y`, `T`, `H`, `H_error`, `alpha`, `G`, `S`, `S_error`).
α < 0 means more unlike neighbours than at random (ordering), and α > 0 means
fewer (clustering). The composition is the nearest one the supercell holds,
and the observations record it. Below the expansion's ordering or miscibility
temperature the canonical sample is no longer one solution phase. Any |α| >
0.5 therefore warns, and such temperatures are better left out.

For a clustering nearest-neighbour pair model at y ≈ 0.5, 2000 and 2800 K
give α₁ = 0.24 and 0.14, ΔS = 4.7 and 5.3 J/(mol K) against 5.76 ideal, ΔG
below the Gibbs–Bogoliubov bound ΔH_random − TΔS_ideal, and −dΔG/dT equal to
ΔS within 5%. Fitted with `L0 = a + b*T`, the data give b > 0: the entropy
that short-range order removes is what a T coefficient in L carries.

**σ_model** is the error of the calculator itself, which no spread over
structures can see, and it is usually the larger part. In the Al–Cu example,
the SQS spreads are 0.1 to 1.3 kJ/mol, while UMA differs from the assessment
by 0.9 kJ/mol RMS. σ_model is `model_sigma=` in kJ/mol (a number, or a dict by
phase) when given. Otherwise it is the model's ensemble or committee spread,
when `from_ase(..., uncertainty=)` provides one. There is no first-principles
value beyond that: take it from a benchmark of the model, or from comparing a
few values with DFT or experiment.

A value with neither part (a compound's formation energy from one structure,
without `model_sigma=`) raises and asks for one, rather than inventing a
number. A mixing enthalpy with only σ_config is used, with a warning that its
σ ignores the calculator's error. `sigma=` sets σ directly and replaces both
parts. `MLIPResult.table()` lists the degrees of freedom of each σ_config.
After a fit, χ²/dof well above 1 means the σ are too small for the scatter
in the data.

A typical universal-MLIP error of 10–30 meV/atom is 1–3 kJ/mol of atoms,
while formation energies are −10 to −40 kJ/mol and phase boundaries move
noticeably for errors of about 1 kJ/mol. A calculator alone therefore gives a
qualitative diagram. Mixing calculated data with experimental data in one fit
is natural: each point carries its own σ and `source`, and the TDB header lists
the sources.

## Provenance and caching

Every `Observation` carries `source`, the label passed to `from_ase` (for
example `"uma-s-1p1/omat"` or `"EMT"`). Relaxations and SQS are cached by
(source label, structure hash, relaxation settings). `cache=True` keeps them in
the user cache next to the TDB files, and a directory works too. The cache
cannot tell that a model's weights changed: give a changed model a new label.

Everything runs in the calling process, one structure at a time. There is no
job submission.

## Phonons: entropies, heat capacities, H(298) − H(0)

`compute(..., phonons=mlip.PhononOptions(), entropy_sigma=0.3)` computes
phonons by finite displacements (`ase.phonons`, no extra dependency) for every
compound or endmember and for the elements' reference structures. The
thermodynamics are exact sums over a Monkhorst–Pack mesh (Γ excluded), 3 modes
per atom. Then:

- the formation enthalpy is reported at 298.15 K: static energy plus the
  vibrational enthalpy, zero-point energy included;
- `SM_FORM` observations give the entropy of formation at
  `PhononOptions.temperatures` (default 298.15, 600, 900 K), with σ
  `entropy_sigma` in J/(mol K);
- with `heat_capacity=True`, `CPM_FORM` observations do the same for heat
  capacity, with σ `heat_capacity_sigma`.

These are what fix a parameter's T coefficient, which 0 K energies cannot. In
`a + b*T + ...`, the entropy of formation is −b per formula unit's atoms.
`quasi_harmonic=True` repeats the phonons at isotropic `strains` and
minimizes F(V, T) for G(T), which adds thermal expansion. For EMT copper, S(298)
is 30.9 J/(mol K) (experiment 33.15), H(298) − H(0) is 4731 J/mol (the database
uses 5004), and Cv reaches 3R.

A structure with imaginary modes (more than `imaginary_tolerance` of them) is
mechanically unstable. It has no vibrational entropy, so it is flagged and left
out. EMT makes BCC Cu and θ-Al₂Cu unstable in this way. Solution phases
(phonons of SQS or random decorations) are not computed yet.

## Liquids: MD and thermodynamic integration

A liquid has no lattice, so SQS and cluster expansions do not apply.
`compute(..., liquid={"LIQUID": [0.25, 0.5, 0.75]}, md=mlip.LiquidOptions(...))`
runs molecular dynamics with the calculator instead, at each of
`LiquidOptions.temperatures` (default 1500 K) and at `pressure` (GPa, default
0; `None` keeps the volume fixed), with ASE's Langevin BAOAB integrator and a
hydrostatic barostat. Each run starts from a random decoration of an FCC
supercell of at least `atoms` atoms (default 108) at the Vegard volume, melts
at `melt_factor` × T, equilibrates at T, and samples. Two kinds of data come
out, per mole of atoms:

- `HM_MIX` at T: ⟨H⟩(x) − x⟨H⟩(A) − (1 − x)⟨H⟩(B), with H the potential
  energy plus PV (the kinetic energy cancels), and σ from block averages;
- `GM_MIX` at T, by alchemical thermodynamic integration. One atom is turned
  from B into A along U(λ) = (1 − λ)U_B + λU_A, and ΔF = ∫⟨U_A − U_B⟩_λ dλ
  (Gauss–Legendre, `n_lambda` points) is the excess part of μ_A − μ_B at
  x = (N_A + ½)/N. A polynomial (`degree`) fitted to ΔF at `n_mu` compositions
  from x ≈ 0 to x ≈ 1 integrates, by Gibbs–Duhem, to
  G_xs(x) = ∫₀ˣ p − x∫₀¹ p. The ideal term is added analytically. No absolute
  free energy of either pure liquid is needed, and the atoms' masses only add a
  constant, which cancels. σ comes from the λ-runs' block errors, carried
  through the fit.

With `GM_MIX` and `HM_MIX` at one or more temperatures, a fit determines both
the a and the b of a liquid `L = a + b*T`. The runs are in
`MLIPResult.liquids[phase]` (`LiquidResult`: `x`, `T`, `H`, `H_error`, `G`,
`G_error`, `volume`, `msd`). A run whose mean-square displacement while
sampling stays below `min_msd` (1 Å²) did not melt, and it warns.

This is the expensive part of the pipeline: (2 + grid + `n_mu` × `n_lambda`)
runs per temperature, each `melt_steps + equilibration_steps + steps` MD steps
(the transmutation runs at the same composition share one melt, and each costs
two calculator calls per step). The dynamics are classical, so there are no
quantum nuclear effects. For a species-blind Lennard-Jones liquid with a
mean-field term N w x(1 − x), `GM_MIX` is exactly w x(1 − x) plus the ideal
term, a fit recovers L₀ = w, and `HM_MIX` agrees with w x(1 − x) within its σ.
